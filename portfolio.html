Academic Research

Honors Thesis — Southeast Asia

Clark Honors College, UO · 2024–2026 · Advisor: Dr. Anca Tristea

Designed and executed a comparative analysis of minority quality of life across three Southeast Asian case studies: Rohingya communities in Myanmar, highland peoples in Thailand, and migrant workers in Singapore. Synthesized datasets from the World Bank, UNHCR, and ILO alongside peer-reviewed literature to build an analytical framework spanning legal status, labor access, land rights, and health indicators. Navigated competing disciplinary methodologies — economics, human geography, political theory — to produce a unified argument that resists easy conclusions and centers the people most affected by policy failures.

Field Research

Global Education Oregon — Berlin

GEO / UNC–Chapel Hill · Berlin, Germany · June–August 2024

Selected as one of only 15 students for a highly competitive program led by UNC–Chapel Hill Undergraduate Dean Ian F. McNeely, in collaboration with the Clark Honors College. Conducted field research at major political and historical sites — including the Reichstag and the Sachsenhausen Concentration Camp Memorial — to examine how national memory shapes modern democratic policy. Brought an economist's lens to questions about institutional memory, reparative policy, and the long-term social costs of historical erasure. The program sharpened my ability to read political spaces, not just political texts.

Policy Organizing

Yes on Measure 119

Community Organizer · Portland metro area, OR · 2024

Canvassed households across the Portland metro to build support for Oregon's first gig worker organizing rights measure. Developed and delivered economic messaging accessible to multilingual communities — translating policy language into the everyday concerns of workers who had never heard of a ballot measure. Tracked outreach data, coordinated volunteers, and made the case door-to-door that labor rights are not a partisan issue; they are a survival issue. The campaign pushed me to take my academic understanding of labor economics and make it useful at a stranger's doorstep.
